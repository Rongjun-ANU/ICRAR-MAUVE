## 20251125 rMZR Literature

The gas-phase metallicity of star-forming galaxies is strongly correlated with galaxy stellar mass – a trend known as the mass–metallicity relation (MZR)[scispace.com](https://scispace.com/papers/the-relation-between-metallicity-stellar-mass-and-star-48dt0823yh#:~:text=cosmological semianalytic model L,We find that). Observationally, more massive galaxies retain higher oxygen abundances in their interstellar medium (ISM) than low-mass galaxies, a relation first quantified across large samples by \citet{Tremonti+2004} (scatter $\sim0.1$ dex). Beyond this primary trend, many studies have explored a possible secondary dependence on star formation. In the global population, galaxies with elevated star formation rates (SFRs) at a given mass tend to exhibit lower metallicitiesui.adsabs.harvard.edu. This defines a “fundamental” plane linking mass, metallicity, and SFRui.adsabs.harvard.edu, often called the Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR)[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/433/2/1425/1749733#:~:text=,). At low stellar masses ($M_\star \lesssim10^{10}M_\odot$), metallicity drops sharply with increasing SFR, whereas in high-mass galaxies ($M_\star \gtrsim10^{11}M_\odot$) metallicity becomes largely independent of SFRui.adsabs.harvard.edu. In fact, *opposite* trends appear to govern the extremes: low-mass starbursts are markedly metal-poor at fixed $M_\star$, while among the most massive galaxies it is the *quenched* systems that show lower metallicities than their star-forming counterparts[scispace.com](https://scispace.com/papers/the-relation-between-metallicity-stellar-mass-and-star-48dt0823yh#:~:text=cosmological semianalytic model L,We find that)[scispace.com](https://scispace.com/papers/the-relation-between-metallicity-stellar-mass-and-star-48dt0823yh#:~:text=dependent on star formation rate,phase metallicities if their star). This inversion was highlighted by \citet{Yates2012}, who found that the sign of the SFR–metallicity correlation flips at a characteristic stellar mass ${\sim}3\times10^{10}M_\odot$[scispace.com](https://scispace.com/papers/the-relation-between-metallicity-stellar-mass-and-star-48dt0823yh#:~:text=cosmological semianalytic model L,We find that). These global scaling relations suggest a complex interplay between star formation and chemical enrichment across different mass regimes.

 

Substantial theoretical effort has been devoted to understanding the origin of the MZR and its SFR dependence. In the “bathtub” or gas-regulator framework[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/433/2/1425/1749733#:~:text=,)[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/430/4/2891/1101320#:~:text=The physics of the fundamental,X)), galaxies reach a quasi-equilibrium between inflow of fresh gas, outflow of metal-enriched gas, and consumption of gas by star formation[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/433/2/1425/1749733#:~:text=,). Inflows of low-metallicity gas dilute the ISM metallicity while simultaneously fueling new star formation, whereas feedback-driven outflows remove metals and gas from the system[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/433/2/1425/1749733#:~:text=,). A well-regulated balance between gas accretion and winds can naturally produce the observed MZR and FMR[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/433/2/1425/1749733#:~:text=,). In this picture, the anti-correlation between SFR and $Z$ at fixed mass is a signature of recent accretion episodes: a galaxy with an anomalously high SFR has likely experienced a surge of metal-poor gas inflow, leading to a diluted ISM abundanceui.adsabs.harvard.edu[academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/433/2/1425/1749733#:~:text=,). Conversely, if a galaxy’s star formation is temporarily elevated without a corresponding gas accretion event (for instance, due to internal gas rearrangement or a compaction), the ISM may become more metal-enriched than average at that mass. Indeed, simple analytic models can reproduce the FMR by assuming that higher SFRs correspond to higher gas inflow rates and thus lower metallicities, with the opposite limit of low SFR (or gas depletion) resulting in metal buildup[durham-repository.worktribe.com](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/preview/1203593/36192.pdf#:~:text=,rates correlate with lower metallicity). Modern cosmological simulations likewise support this interpretation, finding that fluctuations in a galaxy’s gas supply drive coordinated changes in SFR and metallicity consistent with the observed anti-correlation[durham-repository.worktribe.com](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/preview/1203593/36192.pdf#:~:text=,rates correlate with lower metallicity).

 

With the advent of integral field spectroscopy (IFS) surveys, these scaling relations can be probed on *resolved* scales within galaxies. Projects like CALIFA, MaNGA, and SAMI have mapped the spatial distributions of stellar content, star formation, and metallicity across thousands of galaxies, revealing that the global MZR is mirrored by a local relation between gas-phase metallicity and the stellar mass surface density $\Sigma_\star$[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2158#:~:text=,gas recycling in galaxies%2C both)[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=idence for the existence of,resolved investigations at intermediate redshifts). In other words, individual star-forming regions in a galaxy tend to follow a resolved mass–metallicity relation (rMZR) analogous to the global trend: regions of higher $\Sigma_\star$ (typically in galactic centers or inner disks) have systematically higher oxygen abundances than low-$\Sigma_\star$ regions in the outskirts[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2158#:~:text=,gas recycling in galaxies%2C both). This rMZR appears to be ubiquitous in star-forming discs and holds over several orders of magnitude in $\Sigma_\star$[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=idence for the existence of,resolved investigations at intermediate redshifts). For example, early IFS studies by \citet{Sanchez2013} using CALIFA found a tight $\Sigma_\star$–$Z$ correlation within galaxies, which could even reproduce the integrated MZR when convolved with each galaxy’s stellar mass profile[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2158#:~:text=,gas recycling in galaxies%2C both). Similarly, \citet{BarreraBallesteros2016} analyzed hundreds of thousands of MaNGA spaxels and confirmed that local metallicity is primarily governed by local mass density, with a remarkably small scatter (~0.05–0.1 dex) around the rMZR[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=idence for the existence of,resolved investigations at intermediate redshifts). This evidence suggests that the processes establishing the MZR are at play on local scales: metal enrichment and retention are closely coupled to the depth of the local potential well (as traced by $\Sigma_\star$) throughout the galaxy disk.

 

A natural question is whether the **resolved** metallicity also depends on local star formation activity, analogous to the global FMR. Early results were mixed. Several studies reported little to no secondary correlation with the local SFR surface density ($\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$) at fixed $\Sigma_\star$. For instance, \citet{Sanchez2013} found that once the correlation with $\Sigma_\star$ is accounted for, any residual trend with local SFR is negligible[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2158#:~:text=,gas recycling in galaxies%2C both). They showed that even using $\sim$3000 individual H II regions, metallicity variations were driven almost entirely by $\Sigma_\star$, with no significant dependence on Hα-derived $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ except that induced by the $\Sigma_\star$–SFR relation itself[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2158#:~:text=,gas recycling in galaxies%2C both). Likewise, \citet{BarreraBallesteros2016} did not find clear evidence for a resolved FMR in their MaNGA data, concluding that the two-dimensional $\Sigma_\star$–$Z$ relation is largely insensitive to local SFR[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=idence for the existence of,resolved investigations at intermediate redshifts). Using <100 pc observations from the MUSE Atlas of Disks, \citet{ErrozFerrer2019} also reported that the rMZR shows *no* appreciable secondary dependence on $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ on sub-kpc scales[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04493#:~:text=mass of the galaxies,resolved relations have a local). These studies suggested a scenario in which the local metallicity is “decoupled” from instantaneous star formation activity, instead reflecting a longer-term equilibrium set by local mass assembly and recycling. If true, this would imply that the global FMR might emerge from galaxy-scale gas cycling or feedback (e.g. central starbursts or mergers diluting metallicity) rather than from purely local SF–metallicity coupling.

 

More recent work, however, has begun to uncover subtle – but systematic – residual trends that point to a **spatially resolved FMR (rFMR)**. \citet{Baker2023}, analyzing SAMI and MaNGA galaxies with a partial-correlation approach, demonstrated that local metallicity does exhibit a weak inverse correlation with local SFR when controlling for $\Sigma_\star$[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=spatially resolved and global parameters,and 8%2C confirming an rFMR). In their large sample, spaxels with higher $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ tend to have slightly lower $12+\log(\text{O/H})$ at a given $\Sigma_\star$, contributing an additional $\sim$10–15% of variance in metallicity[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=spatially resolved and global parameters,and 8%2C confirming an rFMR). The effect is small at typical stellar-mass surface densities but grows more pronounced in certain regimes. Notably, a decisive breakthrough came from the MAGPI survey: \citet{Koller2024} examined the rMZR in $\sim z{,\approx,}0.3$ galaxies and found that the sign of the $Z$–$\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ dependence *reverses* with local mass surface density[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=match at L1763 that covers,At any)[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=at log(Σ∗) ≳ 7,by plotting the metallicity versus). In low-$\Sigma_\star$ regions (outer disks with $\log,\Sigma_\star/M_\odot{\rm kpc}^{-2}\lesssim7.8$), they detected a clear anti-correlation between metallicity and SFR surface density – mirroring the global FMR behavior where intense star formation accompanies metal dilution[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=match at L1763 that covers,At any). In higher-$\Sigma_\star$ regions (inner disks and bulges), this trend flattens and even inverts such that the highest-SFR patches become slightly more metal-rich[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=match at L1763 that covers,At any). Their results indicate that an rFMR exists for lower-density star-forming regions but vanishes or changes sign in dense, metal-rich zones[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=at log(Σ∗) ≳ 7,by plotting the metallicity versus). Consistent findings have been reported in extreme low-mass systems: for example, \citet{Bulichi2023} show that within dwarf galaxies the anticorrelation between $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ and $Z$ is strong, whereas massive spirals show much weaker trends[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20017#:~:text=Bulichi et al,confirmed by the results from). Taken together, these studies paint a picture in which the **impact of local SFR on metallicity is context-dependent** – pronounced in metal-poor, gas-rich environments and minimal or even reversed in the inner regions of massive galaxies.

 

The emerging interpretation is that the resolved SFR–metallicity relation is governed by the balance of metal production vs. dilution on local scales. If a region’s star formation is fed by a transient surge of metal-poor gas (as might occur in outer disks, tidal accretion streams, or interacting galaxies), the immediate effect is a drop in metallicity concurrent with a boost in SFR – an inverse $Z$–SFR correlation[durham-repository.worktribe.com](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/preview/1203593/36192.pdf#:~:text=,rates correlate with lower metallicity). This scenario likely dominates in low-density environments where gas accretion or mixing events can strongly perturb the ISM. By contrast, in high-density regions of a galaxy, gas inflow fluctuations are relatively small and the gas is rapidly processed by star formation. Here, a high $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ may correspond to efficient conversion of an existing gas reservoir into stars (e.g. in a spiral arm or circumnuclear starburst) without significant dilution from fresh accretion. The newly formed stars and supernovae promptly enrich the local ISM, yielding a positive or null $Z$–SFR correlation once short mixing timescales are considered[durham-repository.worktribe.com](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/preview/1203593/36192.pdf#:~:text=,rates correlate with lower metallicity). This qualitative picture – broadly consistent with the framework put forward by \citet{WangLilly2021} – ties the sign of the correlation to the origin of the star-forming fuel. Regions where **SFR fluctuations are driven by external gas supply** will exhibit the classic anti-correlation (more fuel → more star formation and lower $Z$), whereas regions where **SFR is elevated due to internal processes** (with less variation in supply) can show concordant high $Z$ with high SFR.

 

In this paper, we investigate these issues using new spatially resolved observations of 14 star-forming Virgo spirals from the MUSE Adoption in Virgo Environments (MAUVE) survey. These galaxies are located in a dense cluster environment, which allows us to probe a wide range of local conditions – from tidally or ram-pressure affected outer disks to massive, enriched inner regions – within a single sample. Thanks to the $\sim$100 pc resolution of our MUSE data, we can robustly separate classical H II regions and discern fine structure in the $Z$–$\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ patterns. We present a detailed examination of the rMZR and its secondary dependence on SFR surface density, reporting a clear mass-dependent inversion in the local $Z$–SFR trend. We further introduce a novel spatial correlation analysis to map where metallicity is locally concordant or discordant with recent star formation, and we interpret our findings in the context of a spatially resolved gas regulator model. By combining resolved data and analytic insights, our study aims to shed light on the physical origin of the correlation and anti-correlation between star formation and metallicity in galactic ecosystems.
