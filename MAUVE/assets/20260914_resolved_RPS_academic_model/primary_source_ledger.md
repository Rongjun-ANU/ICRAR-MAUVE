## 1. NGC 4654 gas compression and stellar feedback

Lizee, T., Vollmer, B., Braine, J., & Nehlig, F. (2021), *Gas compression and stellar feedback in the tidally interacting and ram-pressure stripped Virgo spiral galaxy NGC 4654*, A&A **645**, A111. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202038910); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10531).

Access: full 27-page arXiv manuscript downloaded; sections 8-12 and equation page 11 inspected, including rendered equations. Published article has 26 pages.

Supported result: the northwestern high-H I region has inferred molecular-gas SFE about 1.5-2 times higher, with an estimated CO conversion factor 1-2 times Galactic. The paper models compression, turbulence, molecule formation and feedback together.

Printed equations: (19) vertical turbulent-pressure balance; (20) $\xi\dot\Sigma_*=\Sigma_gv_{\rm turb}^3/l_{\rm driv}$; (21)-(22) cloud crossing/freefall times; (23) SFR closure; (27) $R_{\rm mol}=\Sigma_{H_2}/\Sigma_{HI}\simeq t_{\rm ff}^{cl}/t_{\rm mol}^{cl}$; (28) $t_{\rm mol}^{cl}=\gamma/\rho_{cl}$. Here $\xi$ is SN energy input per stellar mass, $l_{\rm driv}$ the turbulence driving scale, and $\gamma$ the molecule-formation coefficient.

Limits: section 10.1 acknowledges nonstationarity and retains equation (26) with varying accretion rate for convenience. Parameter degeneracies and CO conversion uncertainty matter. The dynamical model omits stellar feedback and underpredicts local SFR.

## 2. NGC 4654 interaction history

Vollmer, B. (2003), *NGC 4654: gravitational interaction or ram pressure stripping?*, A&A **398**, 525-540. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361:20021729); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0211321).

Access: primary abstract and full PDF available; abstract and model context consulted.

Supported result: in the simulations tested, ram pressure alone reproduced a gas tail but failed its kinematics; a tidal encounter alone reproduced stellar asymmetry but failed the extended gas tail. Their combined model explains both and places an encounter with NGC 4639 approximately 500 Myr earlier, with ongoing ram pressure.

Limits: this is an inference from the tested dynamical models, not a directly measured orbit or a demonstration that every possible alternative fails. It explicitly establishes tidal interaction as a confounding physical process for interpreting NGC 4654. No new analytical SF law or equation was extracted.

## 3. Earlier resolved Virgo efficiency study

Vollmer, B., Wong, O. I., Braine, J., Chung, A., & Kenney, J. D. P. (2012), *The influence of the cluster environment on the star formation efficiency of 12 Virgo spiral galaxies*, A&A **543**, A33. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201118690); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0430).

Access: publisher first page and arXiv PDF, especially sections 3-4 and NGC 4654 discussion.

Supported result: molecular-gas SFE is broadly similar to field values; no significant windward molecular-SFE enhancement was found. Three systems show possible windward molecular-fraction increases by 1.5-2. These are NGC 4330, NGC 4501 and NGC 4522 in the body text. NGC 4654 has asymmetric low total-gas SFE regions.

Definitions: ${\rm SFE}_{mol}=\Sigma_{SFR}/\Sigma_{H_2}$, ${\rm SFE}_{tot}=\Sigma_{SFR}/(\Sigma_{HI}+\Sigma_{H_2})$; neither is a cloud-integrated efficiency.

Limits: NGC 4654's outer CO data were too shallow to test its truncation region effectively; its CO quality affected some correlations. Projection complicates windward/leeward ratios in highly inclined systems. The later Lizee study uses deeper CO and need not contradict this earlier measurement.

## 4. Compression can coexist with low molecular-gas SFE

Nehlig, F., Vollmer, B., & Braine, J. (2016), *Effects of environmental gas compression on the multiphase ISM and star formation*, A&A **587**, A108. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201527021); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04883).

Access: full arXiv manuscript downloaded; model and discussion inspected. Crossref independently verified title, volume, article, and publication date 2016-02-26.

Supported result: at roughly 1.5 kpc resolution, NGC 4501 and NGC 4567/68 show increased molecular fractions in compressed gas, yet contiguous compressed regions include low molecular SFE. Tidal and ram-pressure compression can yield similar gas diagnostics.

Printed equations: (3) hydrostatic pressure including gas self-gravity; (15)-(16) cloud freefall and molecule-formation times; (18) $R_{\rm mol}=t_{ff}/t_{mol}$; (19) $f_{mol}=R_{mol}/(1+R_{mol})$; (20)-(23) SN/turbulent-energy, SFR, thickness and viscosity closures. Here $R_{mol}=\Sigma_{H_2}/\Sigma_{HI}$ and $f_{mol}=\Sigma_{H_2}/\Sigma_g$.

Limits: the model treats multiple ISM phases as one turbulent fluid in vertical equilibrium. Section 6 discusses $t_{comp}=\rho/(\partial\rho/\partial t)$ versus $t_{turb}\sim l_{driv}/v_{turb}$: rapid compression may raise support and impede SF. CO conversion variations can mask SFE-Q relations.

## 5. Empirical molecular fraction and hydrostatic pressure

Blitz, L., & Rosolowsky, E. (2006), *The Role of Pressure in GMC Formation II: The H2-Pressure Relation*, ApJ **650**, 933-944. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/505417); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0605035).

Access: full arXiv v1 PDF, sections 2-4 and numbered fits checked.

Printed equations: (11) $R_{mol}=(P_{ext}/P_0)^\alpha$. Equation (12), all galaxies, gives $P_0/k=(3.5\pm0.6)10^4\,\mathrm{K\,cm^{-3}}$, $\alpha=0.92\pm0.07$. Equation (13), excluding three disturbed galaxies, gives $(4.3\pm0.6)10^4$ and $0.92\pm0.10$. $R_{mol}=\Sigma(H_2)/\Sigma(HI)$; $P_{ext}$ is an estimated hydrostatic ISM pressure, external to clouds.

Limits: the pressure estimate assumes locally isothermal layers and gas thinner than stars; adopted gas dispersion is 8 km/s. This is not a measured ICM boundary pressure. Section 4 expects breakdown near individual GMC scales and suggests an equilibrium scale around 400 pc locally. Low metallicity/high UV systems may depart. The three excluded galaxies are NGC 3627, NGC 4321 and NGC 4501. Do not mix equations (12) and (13) normalizations or uncertainty values.

## 6. Pressure-dependent jellyfish SFR model

Safarzadeh, M., & Loeb, A. (2019), *Explaining the enhanced star formation rate of Jellyfish galaxies in galaxy clusters*, MNRAS Letters **486**, L26-L30. [Publisher full text](https://academic.oup.com/mnrasl/article/486/1/L26/5454771); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnrasl/slz053); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05900).

Access: publisher full HTML, sections 2-4 and equations (1)-(5).

Printed equation (1): $P_{tot}=\rho_{ICM}c_s^2(1+\mathcal M^2)\cos\theta$, with $\theta$ between disc angular momentum and galaxy motion, so zero is face-on. Equation (2) gives pre-infall hydrostatic ISM pressure. Equation (3) averages pressure-dependent cloud efficiency over a cloud-mass function between $10^4$ and $10^6\,M_\odot$. The text uses ${\rm SFR}\propto\epsilon M_{cold}$ and assumes $M_{cold}(d)/M_{cold,field}\propto d/R_{200}$.

Supported result: this model reproduces a modest integrated disc-SFR enhancement for selected jellyfish systems.

Limits: the gas-loss profile is an ansatz, not a solved local conservation equation; magnetic shielding against cloud evaporation is essential. The adopted pressure/angular law is this paper's prescription, not a general stress boundary condition. It predicts integrated disc SFR, not a leading/trailing map. Its printed mass-function slope notation should be checked before implementation.

## 7. Thermal and dynamical equilibrium SF regulation

Ostriker, E. C., McKee, C. F., & Leroy, A. K. (2010), *Regulation of Star Formation Rates in Multiphase Galactic Disks: A Thermal/Dynamical Equilibrium Model*, ApJ **721**, 975-994. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/721/2/975); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0410).

Access: full arXiv PDF; sections 2.3-2.8 and summary inspected.

Printed equation (19): $\Sigma_{SFR}=\Sigma_{GBC}/t_{SF,GBC}=(\Sigma-\Sigma_{diff})/t_{SF,GBC}$. GBC means gravitationally bound clouds, not all molecular gas. Equation (18) connects two-phase thermal pressure to SFR; (22) gives the diffuse-dominated limit with $\Sigma_{SFR}\propto\Sigma\sqrt{\rho_{sd}}$ when stellar/dark-matter gravity dominates; (23) interpolates toward the bound-cloud limit. $\rho_{sd}$ is stellar-plus-dark-matter midplane density.

Limits: time/space-averaged equilibrium on approximately kpc scales, with applicability stated for $\Sigma\lesssim100\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}$. Section 2.6 gives equilibration over several tens of Myr and explicitly requires averaging over cloud lifecycles. The model contains no prescribed one-sided ICM wind. Adding ram pressure directly to its pressure equations would be a new closure needing justification.

## 8. Feedback-rich simulations with opposite SF responses

Lee, J., Kimm, T., Katz, H., Rosdahl, J., Devriendt, J., & Slyz, A. (2020), *Dual Effects of Ram Pressure on Star Formation in Multiphase Disk Galaxies with Strong Stellar Feedback*, ApJ **905**, 31. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/abc3b8); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11028); [author institution record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A101f108f-d4ed-4106-b6e0-91b3ae418f8d).

Access: primary abstract and author-institution bibliographic record; full PDF request exceeded browser size limit. No printed equation claimed.

Supported result: radiation-hydrodynamical simulations of a disc in a $10^{11}\,M_\odot$ halo find moderate winds increase total SFR by approximately 40% face-on and 80% edge-on; stronger winds suppress SFR by roughly two within 200 Myr and remove both atomic and molecular gas. Dense tail gas does not necessarily form appreciable young stellar populations.

Limits: these numbers belong to the simulated galaxy and specified wind/feedback experiments. They are not universal pressure thresholds or calibrations for MAUVE. This source supplies a direct counterexample to assuming all molecular gas survives stripping or that compression necessarily increases SFR.

## 9. Analytical gas-removal calculation

Singh, A., Gulati, M., & Bagla, J. S. (2019), *Ram pressure stripping: an analytical approach*, MNRAS **489**, 5582-5593. [Publisher full text](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/489/4/5582/5575211); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stz2523).

Access: publisher full HTML, model equations and discussion inspected.

Printed equations: (2) $F_{ram}=\rho(R_c)V^2(R_c)$ and (3) $F_{res}=2\pi G\Sigma_g(R)\Sigma_s(R)$ are forces per area. Equation (17) integrates an exponential gas disc outside the stripping radius; (18) gives the logarithmic stripping-radius estimate for exponential stellar and gas profiles. $R_c$ is position within the ambient halo; $R$ is radius within the galaxy.

Supported result: orbital angular momentum, galaxy formation epoch, and clumpy gas can increase retained gas relative to the maximum-stripping calculation.

Limits: razor-thin, axisymmetric disc; face-on wind; idealized spherical halo and ambient gas; assumed formation history. This is a stripping calculation with discussion of SF implications, not a resolved molecular/SF response model.
