## Alessandro Boselli

Ciao Rongjun 

I read with attention the draft, and I send you here a few comments that you might want to consider while preparing the revised version. I marked most of them on the pdf. Please feel free to consider them only if you think that they are useful.

As a general comment, the paper is interesting but a bit complex for a first reading for non experts. My only major comment is related to the time variations that you consider in your model since I have the impression that you do not consider the fact that these objects are Virgo cluster members, where gas infall is probably suppressed (although this is on global scales rather than local scales) and on the other hand stripping is occurring on timescales of ~100 Myr. The perturbations induced by the interaction of galaxies with their surrounding environment might remove the gas, or mix it locally, changing gas density etc on relatively short timescales. N4383, which is an outlier here, confirms that environmental driven processes might be important. It would thus be interesting to consider this aspect in the analysis and discussion.  A way of doing that would be to see whether there are systematic differences with molecular gas density and distribution, which might be altered during the interaction, or using a comparative analysis between the most and less perturbed objects of your sample.

In any case congratulation for the nice work

Ciao

Hi Chris, 

Sorry for the delayed reply, and thanks again — you’re right to flag the ambiguity.

Please take your time to read, and feel free to send any further comments whenever convenient (by Feb 1st would be perfect but let me know if you need more time).

In Section 2.3, the independent measurements are performed on the Voronoi-binned spectra (i.e., per Voronoi bin). Each Voronoi bin comprises one or more native 0.2″ spaxels whose spectra are co-added to form a single binned spectrum; measurements are performed per Voronoi bin and then mapped back to the native grid by assigning each constituent spaxel the bin’s derived values. As a result, quantities such as Balmer-line detection/SNR cuts, H II vs non-H II classification, Balmer Decrement, extinction correction, and the derived Σ_SFR & Z_gas are all determined per Voronoi bin, not per the original unbinned 0.2″ spaxels.

Where my wording was misleading is that we then reassign each bin’s derived properties back onto the native 0.2″ grid for map-making: every spaxel in a bin is assigned that bin’s values. In the subsequent analyses (e.g., rMZR trends), we treat these “post-binning spaxels” (i.e., the 0.2″ pixels carrying the binned-spectrum-derived quantities) as the data points; or equivalently, weighting each Voronoi bin by the number of spaxels it contains (in other word, by area it occupies).

So, when I used “spaxel” in Section 2.3, I meant spaxels after binning (post-binning spaxels), rather than the original unbinned spaxels. I will revise Section 2.3 to make this explicit and consistently distinguish “Voronoi bin” (measurement unit) from “post-binning spaxel” (map grid / analysis sampling).
